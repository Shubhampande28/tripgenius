Air India has trimmed the fuel surcharge on its long-haul international routes, effective July 1, 2026, as global oil prices eased from the spike caused by the West Asia conflict earlier this year — a real, if partial, rollback for anyone booking to North America, Europe, the UK or Australia.

## What happened

The surcharge for North America and Australia flights dropped to USD 200 from USD 280 per ticket, and for Europe and UK flights to USD 125 from USD 205, per Siasat. Surcharges on other international routes and all domestic flights were left unchanged. Air India had first imposed these fuel surcharges on April 7, 2026, implementing them from April 10 on long-haul routes, after the West Asia conflict pushed jet fuel costs sharply higher — IATA data cited by the airline showed the global average jet fuel price jumping to USD 195.19 a barrel for the week ending March 27, up from USD 99.40 at the end of February, close to doubling in a month. At the time, surcharges ranged from USD 24 to USD 280 on international routes and ₹299 to ₹899 on domestic fares, reflecting distance.

## Why it matters

Aviation Turbine Fuel typically makes up 40-45% of an airline's operating costs, so a near-doubling of jet fuel prices in a month left carriers with little choice but to pass costs on. The July rollback shows that pressure easing, at least for the routes where India's flag carrier had imposed the steepest surcharges. It's a reminder that these surcharges move both ways — they rose fast when the West Asia conflict spiked fuel costs, and they're now coming down as conditions normalise.

## What this means for your trip

- **Booking Air India to the US, Canada or Australia?** The surcharge is down USD 80 per ticket (roughly ₹6,600 at current rates) from its April peak — check your fare breakdown before assuming the old, higher surcharge still applies.
- **Flying to Europe or the UK?** The cut is USD 80 as well, from USD 205 to USD 125 (about ₹6,600 saved).
- **Other international routes and domestic fares are unaffected** — this rollback is specific to the routes listed, so don't expect the same relief on, say, Southeast Asia or Gulf fares.

> IATA data cited by the airline showed jet fuel prices rising to USD 195.19 a barrel for the week ending March 27, 2026, up from USD 99.40 at the end of February — a near-doubling that first triggered the surcharge.

## The bottom line

Fuel surcharges aren't permanent — they track oil prices, and this cut is proof they can fall as fast as they rose. If you've been holding off on booking a US, Canada, UK, Europe or Australia trip on Air India because of the spring fare hikes, it's worth re-checking prices now.
