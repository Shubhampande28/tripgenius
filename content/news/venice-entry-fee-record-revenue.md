Venice has collected more than €5 million (roughly ₹47 crore) from its controversial day-tripper entry fee so far in the 2026 season, with more than 650,000 visitors having paid up. The city's mayor is now floating a steep hike — up to €50 (about ₹4,700) — on the busiest days.

## What happened

The fee, charged on 60 selected peak days between April and July, requires day visitors who aren't staying overnight to book online in advance, pay between €5 and €10 (roughly ₹470-₹940), and present a QR code at checkpoints around the city, including the main train station. Revenue for the 2026 season has already crossed €5 million, on par with the €5.4 million Venice collected across the whole of 2025 from around 720,000 day-trippers.

Mayor Simone Venturini has proposed raising the fee next year to between €30 and €50 (roughly ₹2,800-₹4,700) specifically on the days when the largest tourist crowds are expected — a significant jump from the current €5-€10 range. Venice introduced the fee in 2024, becoming the first city in the world to charge day-trippers an entry fee, and has expanded the number of covered days each year since: from an initial trial to 54 days in 2025, and 60 days in 2026.

Overnight guests are exempt from the day fee, since they already pay a separate accommodation-based tourist tax; the fee applies from 8:30 am to 4 pm on the designated dates, with free entry outside those hours.

## Why it matters

Venice's resident population has fallen below 50,000 even as the city absorbs roughly 30 million tourists a year, and the entry fee is the city's most visible attempt to manage that imbalance without banning day-trippers outright. The scheme has drawn criticism from residents' groups who say a €5-€10 fee is too small to change tourist behaviour, which is likely part of why the mayor is now pushing for fees ten times higher on peak days.

For Indian travellers, Venice remains one of the most popular stops on a first-time Europe itinerary, and this is a live example of a "day fee" model that other overtourism-hit destinations (several are watching Venice closely) may copy.

## What this means for your trip

- If a Venice day trip is on your Italy itinerary, check the 2026 fee calendar before you go — the €5-€10 charge only applies on 60 specific dates between April and July, so travelling outside those windows, or staying overnight, avoids it entirely.
- Book your QR-code entry pass online in advance; turning up without one on a chargeable day risks an on-the-spot fine reported at roughly $300 (about ₹25,000).
- If you're planning a 2027 trip, budget for the possibility that peak-day fees jump to the proposed €30-€50 range — a meaningful cost difference if you're visiting on a weekend in high season.
- Staying overnight in Venice, even just one night, exempts you from the day fee and spreads your spending into the local economy rather than a same-day dash.

## The bottom line

Venice's day-fee experiment is generating real money and hasn't scared off visitors — 650,000-plus have paid this season alone — which likely means the model, and its price tag, keeps climbing. Build a Venice day trip around the fee calendar, not the other way round.
