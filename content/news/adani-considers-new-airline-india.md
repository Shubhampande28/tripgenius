Adani Group is weighing whether to launch its own airline in India, or alternatively buy a stake in an existing carrier — a move that, if it goes ahead, would put a fourth serious competitor into a domestic market currently dominated by two players.

## What happened

Reuters reporting, cited by Aviation A2Z on July 23, 2026, says the discussions inside Adani Group are still at an early stage, with no airline name, fleet plan or launch date decided. The group is reportedly considering two paths in parallel: starting a new carrier from scratch, or acquiring a stake in an existing one. This marks a reversal from December, when Jeet Adani, director of Adani Airports, said the company had no plans to enter the airline business because commercial aviation "operates on thin profit margins."

Adani has also approached regulators seeking a change to rules that currently restrict airport operators from owning stakes in airlines — a regulatory hurdle that would need to move before any Adani-run carrier could take off given the group's existing airport holdings.

## Why it matters

Indian domestic aviation is heavily concentrated: IndiGo holds roughly 65.4% market share, Air India around 25%, leaving a thin slice for everyone else. That imbalance has been made worse by real operational trouble at the two majors — IndiGo cancelled thousands of flights in December amid pilot shortages, Air India has faced scrutiny since the fatal 2023 Boeing 787 crash, and SpiceJet continues to struggle financially. The Indian government has reportedly encouraged several business groups, Adani included, to consider entering the airline business — a sign policymakers want more competitive tension in a market where two carriers control the overwhelming majority of capacity.

## What this means for your trip

- **Nothing changes for bookings right now.** This is an evaluation, not an announced airline — don't expect new routes or fares in the near term.
- **Worth watching if you fly domestic routes often.** More competition historically pressures fares down and adds capacity on under-served city pairs, but that's years away if it happens at all.
- **The Adani-airport-ownership angle matters.** Because Adani already operates several Indian airports, any airline it launches would need a regulatory carve-out — a signal of how seriously to take the plan as it develops.

## The bottom line

This is a story to bookmark rather than act on. If Adani commits, it would be the most significant new entrant into Indian aviation in years — but for now it's a boardroom conversation, not a booking option.
