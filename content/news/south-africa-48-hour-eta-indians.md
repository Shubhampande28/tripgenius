South Africa has rolled out a new Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) for eligible Indian passport holders, with the country's tourism board saying applications can be processed in as little as 24-48 hours. The system, announced July 27, 2026, replaces the older paper-heavy visa process for short leisure and work trips.

## What happened

The ETA is a fully digital travel approval linked directly to a traveller's passport — no physical visa sticker required. It's meant for visits of up to 90 days for tourism or work purposes and supports multiple entries during its validity, according to Travel Trade Journal and Deccan Chronicle.

To apply, travellers create an account on South Africa's Department of Home Affairs ETA portal, upload the required documents, verify their identity through smartphone-based biometric authentication, pay the fee, and submit electronically. The portal also lets applicants track their application status in real time.

For now, the ETA covers arrivals through three ports of entry: O.R. Tambo International Airport in Johannesburg, Cape Town International Airport, and Lanseria International Airport. South African Tourism says more ports of entry will be added in future phases.

## Why it matters

South Africa has long been a bucket-list destination for Indian travellers — safaris, Cape Town's coastline, and the Garden Route — but the visa process has historically been one of the more paperwork-heavy ones among comparable long-haul destinations. A 24-48 hour digital turnaround, if it holds up at scale, puts South Africa closer to the ease of applying for a UAE or Thailand visa than a traditional embassy-run process.

Neliswa Nkani, Hub Head for MEISEA at South African Tourism, called the new system an important step in streamlining travel planning for Indian visitors, per Travel Trade Journal.

## What this means for your trip

If you're planning a South Africa trip, apply through the official Department of Home Affairs ETA portal directly — avoid third-party agents charging markup fees for a process that's designed to be self-service. Since the ETA currently only covers entry through Johannesburg, Cape Town, and Lanseria airports, book flights that route through one of these three until more ports of entry are confirmed. Keep scanned copies of your documents ready before you start the application, since the biometric verification step moves faster with everything prepared upfront.

## The bottom line

South Africa just made itself meaningfully easier to visit on short notice. With processing measured in hours rather than weeks, expect this to nudge more spontaneous long-haul bookings from Indian travellers who'd previously ruled it out over visa hassle — as long as the promised turnaround times hold once volumes pick up.
