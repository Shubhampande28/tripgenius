Amsterdam's city government wants to raise its overnight tourist tax from the current 12% to 20% by 2030 — a move that would make it one of the priciest cities in Europe just to check into a hotel. The plan, reported by DutchNews.nl and flagged specifically for Indian travellers by Curly Tales, is still working its way through the city council but signals where Amsterdam accommodation costs are headed.

## What happened

Amsterdam currently charges a 12% tax on overnight accommodation, or a flat €15 (about ₹1,650) fee for day-trippers arriving by cruise ship or coach. Under the coalition's proposal, that rate would climb to 16% in 2027, then increase by roughly 1 percentage point every year after, reaching 20% by 2030 — nearly double today's rate, according to DutchNews.nl.

The city is framing the hike as a response to overtourism pressure on housing, public space and city services. Curly Tales reports the city wants visitors to "contribute a fair share towards the city's management and maintenance," with revenue funding cleaner streets, safety and general livability improvements for residents as much as tourists.

## Why it matters

For Indian travellers, Amsterdam is usually a two-or-three-night stop bolted onto a wider Netherlands or Benelux itinerary — and hotel tax is one of those costs that's easy to underestimate until checkout. A jump from 12% to 20% on, say, a €150-a-night hotel room adds roughly €12 (about ₹1,050) extra per night by 2030 compared to today, on top of already-high Amsterdam accommodation rates during peak summer months.

Curly Tales' framing is blunt about the direction of travel: this is explicitly being covered as news that "a trip to Amsterdam could get a little more expensive" for Indian visitors specifically, not just a local Dutch budget story.

## What this means for your trip

- If you're planning Netherlands travel for 2027 or later, factor the incremental tax hikes into your accommodation budget rather than assuming today's 12% rate holds.
- Consider basing yourself just outside central Amsterdam (Haarlem, Zaandam) if the tax differential becomes significant — regional municipalities don't always move in lockstep with the capital.
- Booking earlier doesn't dodge the tax since it's charged at checkout regardless of when you booked, but it does protect you from the underlying room-rate inflation that tends to accompany peak-season demand.

## The bottom line

Nothing changes at check-in tomorrow, but the direction is set: Amsterdam hotel bills are getting a steadily bigger tax line every year through the rest of the decade. Build it into multi-year trip planning rather than being surprised at the front desk.
