The Indian Embassy in Tehran has issued a fresh advisory urging Indian nationals to postpone all travel to Iran, and asking those already in the country to consider leaving while flights are still available. The move comes as US-Iran tensions escalate into open military hostility.

## What happened

In mid-July 2026, the embassy urged citizens to "postpone their plans until the security environment improves" and advised Indians currently in Iran to "exercise extreme caution." The advisory cites heightened military activity across several regions, regional hostilities involving Iran, Israel and the United States, and the risk of missile or drone strikes, airport closures and restricted movement.

Several thousand Indians are believed to still be in the country, including students, workers, business professionals and religious pilgrims. The embassy's guidance recommends keeping travel documents on hand at all times, registering with the embassy, and avoiding military installations, protest sites and other high-risk areas.

This is the third escalation in the embassy's messaging since January 2026, when the tone was general caution. By June it had moved to explicit recommendations to leave, and the latest notice goes further, framing the instability as structural rather than a passing spike in tension.

## Why it matters

Iran isn't a mainstream leisure destination for most Indian travellers, but it does see steady traffic tied to religious pilgrimage circuits, business travel, and Indians working or studying there. An MEA advisory of this kind — telling citizens outright to postpone travel and consider evacuating — is one of the strongest signals the government issues short of a formal evacuation operation.

It also has knock-on effects for anyone routing through the wider Gulf and West Asia corridor. Airspace closures and rerouted flights in a volatile Iran-Israel-US standoff can add hours and cost to itineraries that transit through the region, even for travellers who were never headed to Iran itself.

## What this means for your trip

- If you have any trip booked to Iran, non-essential or not, postpone it — this isn't a "monitor the situation" advisory, it's a "leave now if you can" one for people already there.
- If you're already in Iran, register with the embassy immediately, keep your passport and documents on you, and avoid protests, military sites and border areas.
- If you're flying to a different destination that transits through West Asian airspace, check your airline's advisories closer to departure — carriers have been adjusting routes as the conflict develops, which can shift both timings and fares.
- Travel insurance that excludes "war zones" or "advised-against regions" may not pay out for a trip to Iran booked after this advisory — check your policy wording before assuming you're covered.

## The bottom line

This is not a routine advisory update — it's the Indian government telling its citizens, in effect, to get out while they still can. If Iran or a nearby routing is anywhere in your travel plans this year, build in flexibility and check the MEA's advisory page again closer to your travel date, since the situation is moving fast.
