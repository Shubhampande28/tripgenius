Barcelona and the wider Catalonia region doubled their tourist tax from April 1, 2026, pushing combined nightly charges as high as €12–15 (roughly ₹1,100–1,400) at top-end hotels — one of the steepest visitor levies in Europe, according to idealista.com. Mallorca's separate ecotax keeps rising too, and a handful of other Spanish cities are now drafting their own versions. If Spain is on your itinerary, the tax line on your hotel bill just moved.

## What happened

Catalonia's parliament approved the increase, effective April 1, 2026. The new combined rate — regional plus Barcelona's municipal surcharge — works out to about €12 per person/night at five-star hotels (up from roughly €7 previously) and about €9.50 at tourist apartments, per idealista.com. Barcelona's own municipal top-up is set to keep climbing by €1 a year, from €5 in 2026 to a planned €8 by 2029.

Separately, the Balearic Islands' long-running sustainable tourism tax (ecotax) continues on its own schedule in Mallorca, Ibiza and Menorca, ranging from €1 to €4 per person/night in the May–October high season depending on accommodation category, with luxury stays also carrying a 10% VAT surcharge on the tax itself.

Idealista.com reports that other Spanish cities — Santiago de Compostela, Vigo, Toledo — are introducing or drafting their own tourist taxes, and that 25% of Catalonia's tax revenue is earmarked by law for affordable housing programmes, tying the levy directly to Spain's broader housing-affordability debate.

## Why it matters

Spain remains one of the most-searched European destinations for Indian travellers, and Barcelona in particular is a common first stop on a Spain-Portugal or Spain-France combined itinerary. A doubled nightly tax on top of already rising hotel rates changes the real cost of a multi-night Barcelona stay more than headline hotel prices suggest — a family of four on a five-star stay could now be paying an extra €48-plus (about ₹4,500) a night just in tax versus the old rate.

The exemptions are worth knowing too: children 16 and under are exempt from both the Catalonia and Balearic Islands taxes, which matters for family trip budgeting.

## What this means for your trip

- Rebudget Barcelona and Mallorca legs specifically — the tax now shows as a separate line at checkout and isn't always included in the headline room rate you see while booking.
- If cost is tight, 3-star and holiday-rental options carry meaningfully lower per-night tax than 4- and 5-star hotels under the new tiered structure.
- Travelling with kids under 16 keeps them exempt in both Catalonia and the Balearics — worth confirming at booking if the property applies it automatically.

> Barcelona's combined tourist tax has "doubled to one of the highest in Europe," per the Majorca Daily Bulletin's coverage of the Catalan parliament's vote.

## The bottom line

Spain isn't getting more expensive to fly into, but it is getting more expensive to sleep in — and the trend across Barcelona, Mallorca and now smaller cities points toward more Spanish destinations adding their own version of this tax before your next visit.
