Sri Lanka's tourist arrivals crossed 1.3 million for 2026 as of July 26, with India once again the single largest source market, according to Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) data reported by Daily FT and Newswire. Indian arrivals for the year-to-date now stand at 310,293 — close to a quarter of everyone who's visited the island this year.

## What happened

SLTDA figures show 1,313,974 total arrivals between January 1 and July 26, 2026, with 167,401 of those landing in just the first 26 days of July. India led every other source market by a wide margin, contributing 38,785 arrivals in July alone and accounting for roughly 23.6% of total arrivals for the year, per Newswire's reporting.

The UK came in second for the period cited (18,419 tourists, 11%), followed by the Netherlands (10,540, 6%) and China (10,238, 6%), according to Daily FT — underlining just how disproportionately Indian travellers are driving Sri Lanka's tourism recovery this year.

## Why it matters

Sri Lanka has spent the last few years rebuilding its tourism economy after the 2022 economic crisis, and Indian travellers — helped by short flight times, direct connectivity, and a familiar culture — have consistently been first back in. Daily FT's reporting attributes the July numbers specifically to "extensive direct air connectivity provided by national carrier SriLankan Airlines and Indian low-cost carrier IndiGo."

For Indian travellers, this also means Sri Lanka's tourism infrastructure — hotels, guides, beach resorts, Ayurveda retreats — is scaling back up fast, which can mean more competitive pricing during off-peak windows but tighter availability during peak season as the island approaches pre-crisis visitor volumes.

## What this means for your trip

With India already the top source market, popular circuits like Colombo-Kandy-Galle and the Cultural Triangle are seeing steady footfall from Indian travellers — book accommodation a few weeks ahead if you're targeting the July-August or December peak windows. Direct IndiGo and SriLankan Airlines connections out of several Indian cities keep flight costs relatively low compared to other South or Southeast Asian beach destinations, so it's worth comparing fares across both carriers before booking.

## The bottom line

Sri Lanka's recovery story in 2026 is, in large part, an Indian-traveller story. As long as direct flight capacity keeps up with demand, expect the island to keep climbing up the list of easy, affordable international trips for Indians — and expect prices to firm up as arrivals keep closing in on pre-crisis records.
