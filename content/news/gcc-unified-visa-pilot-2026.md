The Gulf Cooperation Council's long-discussed "one visa, six countries" tourist visa is finally inching toward reality, with a pilot phase now confirmed for Q4 2026 (October-December), starting with a UAE-Bahrain air corridor. If you've ever wanted to combine Dubai, Doha, Riyadh, Muscat, Manama and Kuwait in a single trip without six separate visa applications, this is the scheme to watch.

## What happened

The GCC Grand Tours visa — modelled explicitly on the Schengen system — will let travellers clear immigration once and move between participating Gulf states on a single approval, according to Wego's Travel Blog, which cites regional media and official briefings. The pilot begins with a UAE-Bahrain corridor before the intended six-country rollout across UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman, expected in early 2027.

Estimated pricing floated in regional reporting puts a single-country visa at AED 330-380 (roughly ₹7,500-8,600) and the multi-country "Grand Tour" visa at AED 400-480 (roughly ₹9,100-10,900) — though these figures aren't yet confirmed by any GCC government fee schedule. Gulf News, reporting on the initiative's earlier confirmation from GCC officials, noted the scheme is designed to let a single application, single fee and single decision unlock travel across sovereign borders, much like Schengen does in Europe.

## Why it matters

For Indian travellers, the Gulf is usually visited one country at a time — a Dubai trip, a separate Saudi umrah or leisure visa, a Doha stopover — each requiring its own application, fee and processing wait. A working unified visa would make Gulf multi-country itineraries (say, Dubai plus Muscat, or Doha plus Manama) dramatically simpler and likely cheaper than applying for each individually.

## What this means for your trip

Don't rebuild your 2026 Gulf itinerary around this yet — the pilot only covers a UAE-Bahrain corridor initially, existing visa-on-arrival and standard tourist visa routes remain unaffected in the meantime, and officials haven't locked in final pricing or eligibility rules. If you're planning a multi-country Gulf trip for Q4 2026 or 2027, it's worth checking back closer to your travel dates rather than assuming the unified visa will be live and bookable. For now, book [UAE](/countries/uae) and other Gulf destinations on their existing individual visa processes.

> "GCC governments have not yet published final pricing, stay durations or eligibility rules." — Wego Travel Blog, June 2026

## The bottom line

The GCC Grand Tours visa is real and moving, not vapourware — but it's still a pilot with one air corridor, not a six-country product you can apply for today. Expect the practical, bookable version to land sometime after the Q4 2026 pilot, likely in 2027.
