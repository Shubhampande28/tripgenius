If you applied for an Indian passport, visa, or OCI card in Australia any time after July 1, 2026, you likely hit a wall. A tender dispute in Delhi froze in-person consular services across the country for over three weeks — and they've only just come back online, through six upgraded centres.

## What happened

Indian passport, visa, and consular services across Australia were suspended from July 1 after the previous outsourcing contract expired amid a legal challenge. On July 15, the Delhi High Court set aside the Ministry of External Affairs' tender process for outsourced consular services — covering missions in Canberra, Abu Dhabi, Kuwait, and Singapore — finding that the technical evaluation of bidders "was arbitrary and lacked adequate transparency and consistency."

India's Supreme Court declined to overturn that ruling on July 20 but allowed temporary arrangements so essential services could keep running while a fresh tender process plays out. VFS Global resumed operations on July 23, ending a 23-day disruption that fell during the peak school-holiday travel window, according to [PRESS Insider](https://pressinsider.com/policy/india-restarts-visa-services-in-australia-after-court-dispute/).

Six new Indian Consular Application Centres are now open — in Adelaide, Brisbane, Canberra, Melbourne, Perth, and Sydney — with, per [NRI Affairs](https://www.nriaffairs.com/vfs-global-resumes-indian-visa-services/), "upgraded facilities and infrastructure designed to meet current service requirements and the growing demand for CPV services in Australia." Operating hours are 8:30am–4:00pm, Monday to Friday.

## Why it matters

This wasn't India's e-visa system going down — that stayed live throughout, so tourists applying for an Australian e-visa to visit India weren't affected. The disruption hit the other direction: Indians *in* Australia needing physical services — passport renewals, fresh passports, OCI applications, and police clearance certificates — the kind of paperwork often needed before booking international travel or applying for other visas. The contract in question processes over 220,000 such applications a year.

VFS Global is operating under a temporary court-ordered arrangement while a fresh tender concludes, expected within roughly three months, competing against five other bidders for the permanent contract. That means another disruption isn't off the table if the tender timeline slips.

## What this means for your trip

If you're an Indian citizen in Australia needing a passport renewal, OCI update, or police clearance certificate before an upcoming trip, don't leave it to the last minute — book your appointment at one of the six centres now that services have resumed, and build in buffer time given the tender is still unresolved. If you're planning travel *to* Australia from India, this doesn't affect your Australian visa application at all; it's purely about Indian consular services delivered inside Australia.

## The bottom line

Services are back, but the underlying tender dispute isn't resolved — just parked. Anyone with time-sensitive Indian consular paperwork in Australia should get it done now rather than assume this stays smooth through the next few months.
