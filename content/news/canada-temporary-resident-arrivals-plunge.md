Canada's push to shrink its temporary resident population is showing up sharply in the numbers: new arrivals of international students and temporary workers have collapsed compared to two years ago. For Indians weighing a Canadian study or work permit, it's the clearest sign yet that the easy years are over.

## What happened

New IRCC data, reported July 28, shows temporary resident arrivals to Canada fell by nearly 70% — about 225,000 fewer people — in the January-to-May period of 2026 compared with the same months in 2024, according to [immigcanada.com](https://immigcanada.com/canada-records-sharp-drop-in-temporary-resident-arrivals/).

The breakdown is steeper still by category. New international student arrivals dropped 83%, with just 19,425 recorded between January and May 2026 (only 3,335 in May alone), against a 2026 target of 155,000. New temporary foreign worker arrivals fell 63%, to 77,915 over the same period, against a 230,000 target.

The decline is being driven by deliberate policy: annual study permit caps, tighter verification of acceptance letters, higher financial-proof requirements, a scaled-back 2026-2028 Immigration Levels Plan, stricter Post-Graduation Work Permit eligibility, and lower hiring caps and higher wage thresholds for temporary foreign workers. Ottawa's stated goal is to bring the temporary resident population under 5% of Canada's total population by late 2027.

## Why it matters

India has consistently been Canada's largest single source of international students, so an 83% drop in new student arrivals lands disproportionately on Indian applicants and the consultants, colleges, and families built around that pipeline. The data also points to a structural shift rather than a blip: 57% of new permanent residents so far in 2026 came from within Canada's existing temporary resident pool, up from 44% in 2024 — meaning Ottawa is increasingly converting people already in the country rather than admitting fresh arrivals.

## What this means for your trip

If you're applying to study or work in Canada, budget for materially longer odds than in past years — caps are real, acceptance-letter checks are tighter, and financial-proof thresholds have risen. If you're already in Canada on a study or work permit, the flip side is your standing arguably matters more now: Canada is leaning on converting existing temporary residents to permanent ones rather than bringing in new arrivals, so compliance and continuity count.

## The bottom line

Canada's door for new students and workers is narrower than it's been in years, with 2026 targets built around fewer arrivals, not more. Anyone still planning a Canadian study or work permit application should treat this as a tougher, slower process — and plan documentation and finances accordingly.
