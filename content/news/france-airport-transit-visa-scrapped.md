Indian passport holders connecting through French airports no longer need a separate airport transit visa, effective April 10, 2026. The change, confirmed on the French Ministry of Foreign Affairs' own India site, follows a pledge President Emmanuel Macron made during his February 2026 visit to India — and joins Germany, which dropped its own transit visa rule for Indians earlier this year.

## What happened

Under the update, Indian nationals holding an ordinary passport who remain within the international transit zone of a French airport — most relevantly Paris-Charles de Gaulle, a major connecting hub to the Americas, Africa and the rest of Europe — no longer need to apply for or hold an airport transit visa (ATV). The exemption was formalised through a decree amending France's 2010 transit-visa regulations, published in the Official Gazette on April 9, 2026, per the French Ministry's own statement.

The move follows a six-month pilot scheme that, per VisasNews' reporting, was actually discontinued after just two months before this permanent exemption was put in place. Airlines can no longer deny boarding to Indian passengers over lack of an ATV when they're simply transiting through France's international zone without leaving it.

## Why it matters

Airport transit visas have long been an under-the-radar friction point for Indian travellers routing through Europe on connecting itineraries — required even when passengers never intend to leave the airport or cross into the Schengen area itself. Germany scrapped its equivalent rule for Indians via Frankfurt and Munich earlier in 2026; France doing the same now removes a second major European connecting hub from the list of transit-visa headaches.

This matters most for travellers booking budget or mid-range fares that route through Paris CDG en route to onward destinations like the Caribbean, West Africa, or Latin America — itineraries that were previously complicated by needing to secure a transit visa purely to change planes.

## What this means for your trip

- If your itinerary connects through Paris CDG without leaving the international zone, you no longer need to apply for or carry a French airport transit visa.
- This exemption is specific to staying within the international zone during the layover — it does not replace a Schengen visa if you actually plan to exit the airport, or enter France or the wider Schengen area.
- Keep your regular Schengen visa application on the same timeline if France, or anywhere in the Schengen zone, is an actual stop on your trip rather than just a connection.

> The exemption follows France's own decree amending its 2010 transit-visa rules, "published in the Official Gazette on April 9, 2026," per the French Foreign Ministry's statement to Indian travellers.

## The bottom line

One more European hub off the transit-visa list makes connecting through Europe marginally simpler for Indian travellers chasing cheaper fares via Paris. It doesn't touch the actual Schengen visa requirement for anyone planning to visit France itself — that process is unchanged.
