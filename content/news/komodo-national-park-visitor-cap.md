Indonesia is rolling out a hard cap of 1,000 visitors a day at Komodo National Park, phasing in through 2026 after visitor numbers topped 300,000 in 2024 and left the park's famous Padar Island hiking trail resembling, in one official's words, a "busy marketplace."

## What happened

According to Director General Satyawan Pudyatmoko, the surge in visitors has created "potential threats of degradation to both terrestrial and marine ecosystems, as well as discomfort during tourist activities" — prompting the cap. Environment Minister Raja Juli Antoni was blunter about conditions on Padar Island, saying overcrowding there contradicted the park's conservation goals under its UNESCO World Heritage Site status.

The rollout follows a phased timeline: public outreach and simulations ran from October to December 2025, a trial period began in January 2026, and the quota system is set to become fully effective by April 2026. Under the new system, the daily 1,000-visitor cap is split into three sessions of roughly 300-330 people each, with entry allocated based on carrying-capacity studies and managed through a mobile app called SiOra, which handles registration and distribution of slots.

## Why it matters

Komodo is one of Indonesia's marquee wildlife destinations — the only place on Earth to see wild Komodo dragons — and sits close to Bali and Labuan Bajo, both firmly on the Indian traveller's Southeast Asia circuit. A hard daily cap, enforced through an app-based booking system, is a meaningfully different experience from the walk-up access many travellers are used to at Indonesian attractions.

It's also part of a broader regional pattern: destinations from Venice to Bali's own beaches are experimenting with visitor caps and booking systems to manage overtourism, and Komodo's quota, once fully live, will be one of the stricter versions of that model in Asia.

## What this means for your trip

- If Komodo is on your Indonesia itinerary for later in 2026 or 2027, expect to need advance registration through the SiOra app rather than turning up on the day — build that step into your planning, especially in peak months.
- Trips built around Padar Island's viewpoint hike should be booked as early as possible once the quota is fully live in April 2026, since a 1,000-person daily cap split across three sessions will fill faster than uncapped access ever did.
- Pair a Komodo trip with Labuan Bajo as a base — it remains the main gateway and isn't subject to the park's own quota, only the park entry itself.
- If your dates are flexible, shoulder-season visits (avoiding the busiest window travelers are used to) are likely to get you a session slot with less lead time.

## The bottom line

Komodo is moving from open access to a managed, app-booked quota system — good news for the ecosystem, but it means the spontaneous "just show up" version of this trip is disappearing. Anyone planning a visit from 2026 onward should build in an advance booking step.
