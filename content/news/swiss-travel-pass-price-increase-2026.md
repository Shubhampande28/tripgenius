The Swiss Travel Pass, the go-to ticket for foreign visitors covering trains, buses and boats across Switzerland, got roughly 5% more expensive in 2026 — its first price increase since 2023, according to NewlySwissed. The Swiss Half Fare Card, a cheaper alternative popular with longer-stay visitors, rose more sharply, from CHF 120 to CHF 150.

## What happened

After holding flat from 2023 through 2025, the Swiss Travel Pass (Flex) saw an average 5% price bump for passes with a 2026 start date, per NewlySwissed's pricing breakdown. Second-class pricing now runs from CHF 254 (about ₹24,000) for a 3-day pass up to CHF 499 (about ₹47,000) for 15 days; first-class options range from CHF 405 to CHF 787 across the same durations. For comparison, the 3-day second-class pass cost CHF 244 in 2025 — roughly a 4% increase on that specific product.

The Half Fare Card, which gives half-price tickets on most Swiss public transport rather than unlimited travel, jumped from CHF 120 to CHF 150 — a steeper, 25% increase. Travel Switzerland attributes the broader adjustment to growing demand from international visitors and Switzerland's continued popularity as a travel destination, though neither source details specific operational cost drivers behind the hike.

## Why it matters

Switzerland is an expensive country to move around by design — the appeal of the Swiss Travel Pass has always been bundling scenic rail routes (Glacier Express, Bernina Express connections, lake steamers) into one prepaid ticket rather than paying full point-to-point fares, which can run into hundreds of francs for a single scenic route. A 5% pass increase, and a steeper 25% jump on the Half Fare Card, changes the math for travellers who'd planned to buy the cheaper card and pay per-ticket.

For Indian travellers building a multi-country Europe itinerary that includes Switzerland's rail network — often the single priciest transport leg of the whole trip — this is a real, if modest, added cost to plan around.

## What this means for your trip

- If you were planning to rely on the Half Fare Card for a longer Switzerland stay, re-run the math against a full Swiss Travel Pass — the price gap between the two options narrowed with this increase.
- Point-to-point and day-pass products in Switzerland's "Direct National Transport" range were left unchanged in this round, per Travel Switzerland, so short, single-route days may still be cheaper bought individually rather than via a multi-day pass.
- Lock in your pass purchase before any further price move if you already know your Switzerland travel dates — passes are typically bought in advance of the trip rather than in-country.

> The Swiss Travel Pass saw its first price increase "since at least 2023," according to NewlySwissed's 2026 pricing analysis, alongside a jump in Half Fare Card pricing from CHF 120 to CHF 150.

## The bottom line

Switzerland's rail passes are still the most efficient way for visitors to cover its scenic routes without per-ticket sticker shock, but 2026 is a genuinely pricier year to buy one. Compare the Half Fare Card against a full multi-day pass before booking rather than defaulting to whichever option you used on a previous trip.
