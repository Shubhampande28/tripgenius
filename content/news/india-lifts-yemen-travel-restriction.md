India has formally withdrawn a nine-year-old gazette notification that restricted travel to Yemen, though the Ministry of External Affairs is still telling citizens to avoid non-essential trips there. It's a rare loosening in a region where Indian advisories have mostly been tightening this year.

## What happened

The Government of India's original Gazette Notification No. 3223(E), issued on October 3, 2017 to formally restrict travel to Yemen, has been withdrawn via a new Gazette Notification No. 3760(E) dated July 10, 2026. The Ministry of External Affairs made the move after conducting a review of the travel restrictions that had been in place for nearly a decade, according to reporting citing ANI.

Despite lifting the formal restriction notification, the MEA's guidance is largely unchanged in substance: it continues to advise citizens to "refrain from non-essential travel to Yemen." Anyone whose travel is unavoidable is told to conduct a thorough security assessment beforehand and to avoid active conflict zones altogether. Indians who are in or travelling to Yemen are advised to register with the Embassy of India in Riyadh, which handles Yemen-related consular matters, for emergency assistance and communication.

## Why it matters

Yemen has been under some form of Indian government travel restriction since 2017, largely due to its ongoing civil conflict. Formally withdrawing the notification is a procedural, not a practical, shift — it removes a specific bureaucratic restriction rather than declaring the country safe. For the small number of Indians with ties to Yemen (largely expatriate workers, rather than tourists), it may ease some administrative friction, but the underlying security guidance is unchanged.

It's also a useful reminder that MEA advisories operate on two tracks: formal gazette notifications restricting travel, and the separate, more frequently updated advisory guidance. A change to one doesn't necessarily mean a change to the other, as this case shows directly.

## What this means for your trip

- Yemen isn't a tourism destination for Indian travellers in any meaningful sense, and this change doesn't alter that — the "avoid non-essential travel" guidance still stands.
- If you do have unavoidable travel to Yemen (work, family, or official reasons), register with the Embassy of India in Riyadh before and during your trip, and keep a documented security assessment on file.
- The broader lesson applies to any destination advisory you're checking before booking: read the current guidance itself, not just whether a restriction notification exists or has been withdrawn — the two can move independently.

> The Ministry of External Affairs "continues to advise all Indian nationals to refrain from non-essential travel to Yemen," even after formally withdrawing the 2017 restriction notification.

## The bottom line

This is a paperwork change, not a green light — Yemen stays firmly in "avoid unless essential" territory for Indian travellers, and the formal withdrawal mainly simplifies the MEA's own regulatory bookkeeping rather than opening the country up.
