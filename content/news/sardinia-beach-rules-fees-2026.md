Sardinia has introduced a patchwork of entry fees, daily visitor caps and steep fines across its most photographed beaches this summer, with penalties reaching up to €3,500 (about ₹3.3 lakh) at its most protected coves. If a Sardinian beach day is part of your Italy plans, several now require advance booking rather than a walk-up visit.

## What happened

The rules vary sharply by beach. At Spiaggia Rosa on Budelli Island — closed to visitors since the mid-1990s after tourists were caught smuggling away kilos of its distinctive pink sand as souvenirs — fines start above €500 (roughly ₹47,000) for simply walking on the beach and climb to €3,500 (about ₹3.3 lakh) for taking sand.

Elsewhere, access is capped rather than banned. Cala Mariolu allows 700 people at a time with a €2-3 environmental levy, running from June 15 to September 15. Pelosa Beach near Stintino now caps at 1,500 people a day (down from crowds that once hit 4,000), charges a €3.50 (about ₹330) ticket, and requires beach towels to be laid on mats — violations draw on-the-spot fines up to €100 (about ₹9,400). Cala Brandinchi and Lu Impostu, both booked via the San Teodoro app, cap at 1,447 and 3,352 visitors respectively between June 1 and September 30. At Punta Molentis in Villasimius, a new June 2026 rule bans beach umbrellas except for visitors over 65 or travelling with children under 10, limited to one per family, alongside a €10 (about ₹940) entry fee.

Guided-tour-only coves like Cala Coticcio and Cala Brigantina in the La Maddalena Archipelago cap at 60 people a day with a €3 fee, while Ogliastra's Cala Birìala and Cala dei Gabbiani limit both daily numbers (300 each) and boat-arrival stay times to 70-90 minutes.

## Why it matters

Sardinia's move reflects a wider Mediterranean pattern this year — Italy, Greece, Spain, Croatia and France have all tightened beach access rules in 2026 in response to overcrowding and environmental damage. Unlike a single city-wide tourist tax, Sardinia's approach is granular: each beach has its own cap, fee and booking method, which means a rule that applies at one cove may not apply a few kilometres down the coast.

For Indian travellers building a Sardinia stop into a wider Italy trip, this means the "just drive to the coast and find a beach" approach no longer works reliably at the island's best-known spots — several genuinely require you to book ahead or risk being turned away, or fined.

## What this means for your trip

- Check the specific beach's rules before you go — Sardinia doesn't have one island-wide policy; caps, fees and booking apps differ cove to cove.
- Book ahead through the relevant app (San Teodoro's app covers several northern beaches) if your itinerary includes Cala Brandinchi, Lu Impostu or similar capped beaches, especially for a peak-season weekend.
- Skip souvenir sand or shells entirely — protected coves like Spiaggia Rosa treat this as a serious offence with fines running into lakhs of rupees, not a minor slap on the wrist.
- Carry small cash or card for on-the-spot entry fees (mostly €2-10, roughly ₹190-940) at capped beaches that don't require pre-booking.

> Fines for removing sand from Spiaggia Rosa run "up to €3,500" (roughly ₹3.3 lakh), among the steepest beach penalties reported in the Mediterranean this year.

## The bottom line

Sardinia's beaches are shifting from open access to a booked, capped and fined system, cove by cove. Build in 15 minutes of research per beach on your itinerary before you go — it's the difference between a smooth beach day and being turned away, or fined, at the entrance.
