The stereotype of the impulsive holiday booking — see a deal, book it same
day — doesn't match how Indian travellers are actually planning trips
anymore. Booking data increasingly shows a more deliberate process: multiple
fare comparisons, real budget tracking, and a growing willingness to swap the
destination itself if the numbers don't work, rather than cancel the trip
outright.

## What's actually changed

Skyscanner's Smarter Summer Report found that nearly **9 in 10 Indians**
have either planned or already booked their next big holiday, and **77%**
say they're confident about travelling within the next three months — but a
large share, around **38%**, remain unbooked and still actively searching
rather than committing early. The more telling number is what happens when
plans hit friction: instead of cancelling, **30% of travellers switch
destinations** while keeping the trip itself intact, adjusting timing or
budget rather than giving up on travelling altogether. As Skyscanner's Travel
and Destinations Expert Neel Ghose put it, "Travellers are not stepping back;
they are adapting within constraints."

Cost-consciousness runs through the same data: **48%** of travellers now
factor fluctuating flight prices directly into their planning, and **45%**
weigh total trip cost more carefully than before. On the destination side,
**81%** say they're open to lesser-known places and **60%** are actively
seeking quieter, less-crowded alternatives to the usual hotspots — echoed by
homestay platform Zostel reporting **88% year-on-year growth** in bookings
to offbeat destinations, with 60% of those being first-time travellers from
Tier 2 and 3 cities.

## Why it matters for budget and planning

- **Price comparison is now a default step, not an extra one** — with nearly
  half of travellers tracking fare fluctuations, waiting and comparing before
  booking has become standard behaviour rather than a money-saving trick.
- **Destination flexibility protects the trip, not just the wallet** — the
  30% who switch destinations under budget pressure are keeping their travel
  plans alive rather than cancelling, which is a meaningfully different
  outcome than in a market where price sensitivity simply kills bookings.
- **Offbeat demand is now big enough to move supply** — an 88% jump in
  offbeat bookings on a single platform signals homestay and hotel capacity
  in those areas will keep expanding to meet it.

## What this means for your trip

Build fare comparison and a flexible shortlist into your planning from the
start rather than fixing on one destination and one price. If your first
choice gets expensive closer to the date, treat swapping to a similar,
less-crowded alternative as a normal part of planning — not a downgrade —
since that's exactly what a large share of Indian travellers are already
doing successfully.

> "Travellers are not stepping back; they are adapting within constraints."
> — Neel Ghose, Skyscanner.

## The bottom line

The shift isn't that Indians are travelling less — it's that they're
planning more carefully: comparing fares, watching total cost, and swapping
destinations rather than cancelling trips. That more deliberate approach is
becoming the default, not the exception.
