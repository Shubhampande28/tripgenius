For years, the default Indian domestic trip list barely changed — Goa,
Manali, Shimla, on repeat. That's shifting. Flight-search data is now showing
a genuine, sustained pull toward destinations that were barely on the map a
few years ago, and it's not a one-season blip — it's a pattern that keeps
showing up run after run of travel-trend data.

## What's actually changed

Skyscanner's 2026 travel trends report puts a number on it: **Jorhat, Assam**
saw a **493% year-on-year surge** in flight searches, making it the single
fastest-growing destination among Indian travellers, ahead of every
traditional hill-station or beach favourite. **Varanasi** wasn't far behind,
with a **120% increase** in flight searches, landing it among the top ten
trending destinations nationally — notable because Varanasi has historically
been viewed narrowly as a pilgrimage stop rather than a leisure destination.
Neel Ghose, Skyscanner's Travel Trends Expert, framed the shift plainly: "One
in three Indian travellers are avoiding crowded, tourist-heavy locations in
favour of authentic alternatives."

Jorhat's pull is specific — it's positioned as Assam's cultural capital and
the gateway to Majuli, the river island, with tea estates and Vaishnavite
monasteries drawing travellers who want an experience beyond a checklist
photo stop. Varanasi's re-framing is just as telling: travellers are now
booking it for riverfront heritage walks and boutique stays, not only
religious visits.

## Why it matters for budget and planning

- **Less competition for rooms and seats** — destinations still ramping up on
  search volume haven't seen airfares and hotel rates inflate the way
  saturated hotspots have.
- **A real, data-backed shift, not a niche trend** — a 493% search jump is a
  broad enough signal that it changes route capacity and hotel supply over
  time, not just a handful of early adopters.
- **Off-the-checklist appeal is the draw, not price alone** — travellers are
  actively seeking fewer crowds and more authentic experiences, which means
  these picks reward travellers who want a different kind of trip, not just
  a cheaper one.

## What this means for your trip

If your usual shortlist is starting to feel repetitive, treat this as
permission to look one tier down from the obvious pick. Northeast India in
particular keeps producing these breakout numbers — Jorhat and Majuli today,
likely another district tomorrow — because connectivity and homestay
capacity are still catching up to demand, which means better availability
and value for travellers who go before the rest of the search volume does.

> "One in three Indian travellers are avoiding crowded, tourist-heavy
> locations in favour of authentic alternatives." — Neel Ghose, Skyscanner.

## The bottom line

The shift away from the same five domestic hotspots isn't a passing season —
it's showing up consistently in search and booking data, and Northeast India
keeps landing at the top of it. Travellers who follow the search data instead
of the old shortlist are finding better value and fewer crowds for it.
