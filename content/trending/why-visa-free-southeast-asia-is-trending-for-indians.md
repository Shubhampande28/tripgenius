Southeast Asia has quietly become the default first-international-trip answer
for Indian travellers, and it's not by accident. Malaysia, Sri Lanka,
Indonesia and Vietnam all keep low-friction entry for Indian passport
holders — removing the single biggest friction point in international
travel: the paperwork. (Thailand is the exception here: it rolled back its
60-day visa-free entry for Indians in mid-2026 — see [Thailand's new entry
rules](/news/thailand-ends-visa-free-entry-indians-new-rules) if Bangkok or
Phuket is still on your list.)

## What's actually changed

For a long time, Indian travellers planning a first trip abroad ran into the
same wall: a visa application that ate weeks of lead time, a stack of
documents, and a fee that made a budget trip feel expensive before it even
started. Visa-free or near-visa-free access flips that. A traveller can now
decide on a Colombo or Bali trip two weeks out and still make it happen.

- **Malaysia** — visa-free entry for Indian passport holders extended through 31 December 2026, up to 30 days for tourism.
- **Sri Lanka** — free tourist ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) for Indian nationals under its current tourism-promotion push.
- **Indonesia (Bali)** — visa-on-arrival, fast and inexpensive.
- **Vietnam** — e-visa process simplified and widely used by Indian travellers.
- **Thailand** — the outlier: visa-free entry ended in 2026, replaced by Visa on Arrival with added cash and paperwork requirements.

## Why it matters for budget and planning

Removing the visa step doesn't just save paperwork — it changes the shape of
the trip. Shorter lead times mean travellers can chase flight-price dips
instead of planning around visa processing windows. It also nudges first-time
international travellers toward destinations that are visa-free by default,
rather than picking a destination and then discovering the visa cost eats
into the budget.

## What this means for your trip

If you're planning a first international trip, low-friction destinations are
the easiest way to start — no waiting on embassy appointments, no
non-refundable visa fees if plans shift. Check [Bali](/cities/bali) and
[Sri Lanka's best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit/colombo) before you lock
in dates, since shoulder-season pricing still swings by month even on
visa-free or on-arrival entry.

> Visa-free access is often the deciding factor between "someday" and "booked
> this month" for first-time international travellers.

## The bottom line

Visa-free Southeast Asia isn't a one-off news story — it's a standing shift
in how Indian travellers pick their first trip abroad, and it keeps
influencing which destinations trend on TripGenius month after month.
