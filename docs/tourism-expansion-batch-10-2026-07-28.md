# Tourism coverage audit and researched expansion batch

Prepared: 28 July 2026

Audience: TripGenius product, SEO and editorial teams

Batch: Europe gap batch 1 (10 cities + 10 city-linked articles)

## Executive finding

Before this batch, TripGenius had 483 unique cities and 93 unique blogs. Eighty-four
blogs were connected to at least one city. The raw city count looked strong, but the
portfolio was unbalanced: India represented 219 cities (45.3%), while several
high-utility European countries had no country hub or city guide at all.

The most important problem was therefore not “too few places” in the abstract. It
was missing destination clusters that travellers plan together. Slovenia, Serbia
and Montenegro are common multi-stop itineraries, yet none of their core cities
existed. Varna, Bratislava and Tallinn were also obvious holes in Black Sea,
Central European and Baltic coverage.

This batch raises the verified totals to:

- 493 unique city records
- 103 unique blogs
- 94 city-linked blogs
- 10 new local Wikipedia/Wikimedia lead images
- 6 new country hubs

The destinations were delivered as clusters instead of isolated pages:

- Slovenia: Ljubljana, Lake Bled and Piran
- Serbia: Belgrade and Novi Sad
- Montenegro: Kotor and Budva
- Bulgaria: Varna
- Slovakia: Bratislava
- Estonia: Tallinn

## Editorial and verification method

1. Existing slugs, country membership and blog links were checked against the
   runtime data exports, not inferred from filenames.
2. Destination claims and visitor sequencing were checked against official
   municipal or national tourism sources.
3. Visa wording was checked against government or embassy sources and written
   conservatively. Every article tells readers to re-check before booking.
4. Volatile prices and exact timetables were excluded unless they were necessary
   to explain how a system works. Articles warn readers when rail operations,
   seasonal boats, border delays or attraction access can change.
5. Every article is attached to exactly one city slug, includes four FAQs, and
   links to its city guide.
6. Images were downloaded from Wikipedia/Wikimedia, stored locally, and recorded
   with creator and licence metadata in
   `audit/tourism-batch-10-wiki-images.json`.

## Remaining high-value gaps

The next city expansion should continue in batches of ten. Recommended order:

1. Baltic and western Balkans: Riga, Vilnius, Sarajevo, Mostar, Skopje, Ohrid,
   Tirana, Berat, Gjirokastër and Sarandë.
2. Mainland China gateway batch: Beijing, Shanghai, Xi’an, Chengdu, Guangzhou,
   Guilin, Hangzhou, Suzhou, Zhangjiajie and Lijiang.
3. High-intent North America and Mexico: Orlando, Boston, Seattle, New Orleans,
   Honolulu, San Diego, Niagara Falls, Whistler, Cabo San Lucas and Mérida.
4. Under-covered Africa: Addis Ababa, Lalibela, Accra, Cape Coast, Tunis,
   Sidi Bou Said, Algiers, Kampala, Victoria Falls and Livingstone.

The next editorial priority should be city-linked blogs for existing cities that
currently have no authored article. New city creation should pause whenever the
ratio of city-linked blogs falls, because additional thin destination inventory
does less for travellers than a smaller number of well-researched planning pages.

## Publishing and legal notes

- Wikimedia image licences include CC BY and CC BY-SA variants. Preserve the
  creator, licence and source records in the JSON audit file and surface image
  attribution wherever the production design requires it.
- These new city records intentionally rely on the project’s synthetic fallback
  modules for some secondary sections. Under the existing indexing rule, they
  remain excluded from city/month sitemap indexing until hand-authored monthly
  weather, budgets and other depth modules are added.
- The ten blogs are authored, city-linked pages and are eligible for normal blog
  indexing. Their transport and entry wording is designed to age safely, but each
  should still enter the normal editorial re-verification cycle.
